A PART row carrying its caption

Round 5 closed one path into _open_caption_chapter. The rules lane still had anonymous containers, and they turned out to be PART headings:

_roman_value sums a suffix's letters

insert_missing_body_chapters ends by sorting every chapter by _roman_value, which folds a suffix into two decimal places by adding up its letter values. The collisions are immediate:

ChaptervalueChaptervalue
XIV-AA14.02=XIV-B14.02
XIV-AB14.03=XIV-BA, XIV-C14.03
XIV-AC14.04=XIV-BB, XIV-D14.04

Sorting on that interleaved two families:

tree     XIV-B  XIV-AB  XIV-BA  XIV-C  XIV-AC  XIV-BB  XIV-D  XIV-AD
contents XIV-AB XIV-AC  XIV-AD  XIV-B  XIV-BA  XIV-BB  XIV-C  XIV-D
         ..105  ..105   ..107   ..111  ..125   ..132   ..136  ..140

Chapters printed on pages 123–125 came out after ones on 129–158 — exactly what structure_counts reports. A suffix is a sequence, not a sum. Ordering on (base numeral, suffix letters) reproduces the source order exactly.

_roman_value itself is unchanged. It is correct for the nearest-previous-chapter search it was written for, and round 1's Arabic/roman guard depends on its current behaviour. Only the sort key is new.

The tiebreak that had to come out

The first version added ch.code as a final tiebreak and got XIVA and XIV-A backwards. They share a key, and a string compare orders them by punctuation — - sorts before A — rather than by the contents page, which lists XIVA first. Round 1 established those are two different chapters.

list.sort is stable, so with no tiebreak equal keys keep the TOC's own order — which is the authority here.

Both facts are locked: the twelve-chapter sequence, and the XIVA / XIV-A pair that fails if the tiebreak returns.
