The message lied Not an ordering problem. A parsing one.

82A out of order after 224

Section 82A sat at tree position 262 — after the document's last section — while printing on page 91. It was not in CHAPTER IX where it belongs. It was in an anonymous container appended to the end of the tree, and so were sections 167–192, in a second one. The parse reported 24 chapters against a contents page listing 22.

Both containers open at a TOC page break, and both breaks land inside a wrapped row:

82.    Procedure in case of goods not cleared or warehoused or
                              (viii)
             THE CUSTOMS ACT,1969          ← ALL-CAPS: a "foreign caption"
       transshipped or exported or removed from the port within
       twenty days after unloading or filing of declaration.    66

82A.   Omitted.                                                 67   ← lands inside it

The contents page's own running title is capitalised, so it satisfies is_foreign_caption and _open_caption_chapter opens a chapter for it. Everything until the next real chapter heading parents there.

The fix One line. The filter already knew this exact string.

Two call sites, two orderings, one of them right

toc.py has _page_furniture for precisely this line. Its docstring names this document and this section:

The 2007 Customs contents centre "THE  CUSTOMS   ACT,1969" on all 22 of theirs […] so the copy that lands after the last section row of a page was glued to that row — s.82A's heading came out "Omitted THE CUSTOMS ACT,1969".

The set is consulted at two call sites, and they order the tests differently:

Call siteOrder of tests
the last_section pathin furniture → then is_foreign_caption
the pending_page pathis_foreign_caption → then in furniture

The second is the one reached when a page break lands inside a wrapped row — exactly this case — and by then the caption branch has already fired. Test furniture first, as the other site already does.

Customs 2008 now parses 22 chapters, 82A is back in CHAPTER IX (pages 86–91), 167–192 in CHAPTER XVIII, zero anonymous containers, all 297 sections preserved. That edition drops from 5 hits to 2.

Round 1's shape again: not a missing rule, but two places applying the same rule in a different order.

The invariant Measured separately, on identical JSON.

A proxy standing in for what the name says

_CHAPTER_CAPTION_IN_HEADING fires on any run of three or more capitalised words. What the check means is that a chapter's caption leaked into a section heading — and a section whose own title is set in capitals matches the proxy just as well: 

150 ZQZA. RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE VENDOR.–(1) Subject to these rules,

That is Sales Tax Rules 2006 setting that rule's own title in capitals — reported in both editions once round 4 let the rule bind at all. So the check now asks the question its name asks: is this run a caption that appears on a chapter of this document?

The locking fixture was wrong, and that is a finding

The synthetic document that pins this detector has section 14A with PROHIBITION AND RESTRICTION OF IMPORTATION AND EXPORTATION glued to its heading — and no chapter carrying that caption. Under the new check it stopped firing.

Worth stating rather than patching away, because it asks a real question: can the leak happen while the chapter is absent from the tree? It cannot. Customs omits the CHAPTER IV row from its contents, but _open_caption_chapter still opens a node for the bare caption — so the chapter is present, verified on all three editions carrying the defect today.

The fixture was missing the very thing that makes its document a leak. It now includes it, and still fails if that chapter is removed.
