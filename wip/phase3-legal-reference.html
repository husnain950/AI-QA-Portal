The caption that was never in a body

The finding 45 of 45 hits, measured across the whole acts lane.

The invariant was reading markup, not text

Every hit was LEGAL REFERENCE caption in body, on Customs Act sections 79, 81 and 123 and the Second and Third Schedules, across twenty editions. The message is wrong. The caption is not in the prose — it is inside a citation tooltip: 

<sup class="cite" title="Added by Finance Act, 2015&#10;LEGAL REFERENCE">79.8</sup>

inv_no_footnote_text_in_body ran its pattern over the raw html string, so an attribute counted as body text. Measured over the lane: 45 of 45 hits sit inside a title= attribute, and zero appear in rendered text — not in plain_text, not in the HTML outside the attributes.

The check now strips tags first, using the same re.sub(r"<[^>]+>", "", html) idiom _common.py already applies in five other places.

Measured on identical JSON, the invariant fix alone is 193 → 148.
What it hid 473 footnote texts, against 45 reported hits.

A tooltip carries the note it points at

So the caption being in the tooltip meant the caption was in the footnote's own text. That is a real defect, and a far bigger one than the register ever showed: LEGAL REFERENCE sat in 473 footnote texts across 20 Customs editions.

The path was two steps, each individually reasonable. The page has a single horizontal cut separating body from footnotes. The caption is set at body size, so the cut lands below it — leaving apparatus in the prose. Step one rescued it across. Step two then read a line sitting above the first marker the only way it knows how: as the tail of a note continued from the previous page.

Before — rescued across the cut

14. Stations for officers of customs…
Y-cut
LEGAL REFERENCE  → ^cont
19. Substituted by Finance Act, 2013…

After — dropped from both

14. Stations for officers of customs…
Y-cut
LEGAL REFERENCE
19. Substituted by Finance Act, 2013…

A pre-marker line is, by definition, a continuation — so the caption came back as a ^cont fragment and was spliced onto the previous page's last note. A caption printed above the notes was appended to the note before it, and every leaf citing that note inherited it.

The caption is neither statute nor a note. It now belongs to neither zone, dropped from both by _drop_apparatus_captions, whichever side of the cut it landed on.

The layer decision The shorter fix was in the wrong place, and the conservation audit said so.

Where the guard goes changes what it costs

The first version guarded parse_footnotes: skip a caption when building notes, leave it in footnote_lines. It worked — 473 to nil — and it was wrong. audit_completeness.py compares the page model's footnote_lines against the output's footnote texts, so a caption present on one side and absent from the other is, to the audit, lost text.

The 48 were LEGAL: 24, REFERENCE: 21, REFERENCES: 2, REFERENCS: 1 — the caption itself, in all three printed spellings the Customs Act uses.

Dropping it one layer earlier costs nothing on either side and is the shorter diff: it replaces the old move-between-zones block rather than adding to it, and it lets footnotes.py and grammar.py leave the changeset entirely. Source footnote tokens went 21,369 → 21,321 — the 48 now counted on neither side.

The lock moved with the fix. It asserts on _drop_apparatus_captions, the seam the behaviour actually lives on — verified to fail when the filter is stubbed out. Locked at parse_footnotes, the earlier version's test would still have passed.

Incidental A skip inside a function cannot report itself stale.

One tracked gap that had already closed

The check carried a hardcoded skip for a single (edition, leaf) pair — Income Tax Ordinance 11.03.2019, Division XXI, whose substituted rate table once rendered inside the division body.

Re-measured, that leaf matches no branch of the check: no marker text, no caption, in neither plain_text nor html, before this PR as well as after. Earlier work had closed it and nothing noticed.

That is the argument for the repo's own format. An entry in tools/suite/exemptions/ prints “exemption is now stale — delete the entry” the moment its invariant starts passing. A skip buried in a function is silent forever. Deleted rather than moved, because there is nothing left to exempt.
