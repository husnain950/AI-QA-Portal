One numeral, three readings

Phase 2 left five items open. The first turned out to be three defects with one shape — the body scan, the document tree and the invariant each recognised a chapter numeral differently. Closing it returned two documents the parser had been refusing outright, deleted nineteen chapters that were never in the statute, and took the register from 210 to 193.

1 · A heading wearing a footnote marker was invisible

The two documents Phase 2 lost were not lost to the profile change and had nothing to do with OCR. The Sales Tax Act, 1990 prints its first chapter like this:

4 [Chapter-I

An amendment footnote marker, in front of the bracket. grammar.CHAPTER_RE tolerates the bracket — a comment records it being widened for exactly that — but not the marker ahead of it. So CHAPTER I never registered while Chapter-II through Chapter-X did; sections 1 and 2 were left with no container, and the pipeline refused the whole document rather than drop them:

RuntimeError: TOC parse left 2 section(s) without a chapter container (1, 2...)

That refusal is right — legal text must not silently vanish — so the document produced nothing at all, and Phase 2 quarantined the JSON an older revision had written.

The fix is one expression, and the function was already importing what it needed. body_chapter_entries calls is_structural_boundary three lines below the match it was getting wrong, and that helper reads its line through _STRUCT_DECOR_RE precisely because 1[PART VA is a real heading. The chapter match now reads the line the same way. Both editions convert again — 10 chapters, 126 and 124 sections.

2 · CHAPTER 1 and CHAPTER I are the same chapter

The Customs Act 1969 prints its first chapter heading with an Arabic 1 on page 23, and roman numerals everywhere else — including on its own contents page. insert_missing_body_chapters matched numerals as strings, found nothing for 1, and built a chapter from scratch:

tree chapter codes: ['CHAPTER 1', 'CHAPTER I', 'CHAPTER II', ...]
                      ^^^^^^^^^ 'PRELIMINARY', 0 sections
                                  duplicate of 'CHAPTER I' 'PRELIMINARY', 2 sections

Nineteen editions, nineteen empty chapters — and the reason every Customs edition reported 23 chapters against a contents page that says 22. They now report 22.

The obvious generalisation drops a real chapter

Comparing _roman_value, which already reads both notations, is the one-line version — and it is wrong. XIVA and XIV-A share a value but are two different chapters of Sales Tax Rules 2006: the first omitted, the second the monitoring chapter. structure_counts’ own comment already says so — “the dash is the only thing telling them apart”.

Found by reading that comment, mid-run, with the first re-conversion already going; it was stopped and restarted. The match now bridges only the notation gap, guarded by isdigit() so same-notation numerals still compare as strings, and the non-collapse has its own locking case. Verified on the document: 43 chapters, both present.

3 · Two normalisers, one comparison, no agreement

inv_body_chapters_in_tree asks whether every chapter the body prints exists in the tree. It normalised the two sides with different functions:

_tree_chapter_numeral("CHAPTER VIA")  ->  'VI-A'
_norm_body_numeral("VIA")             ->  'VIA'

So a chapter whose code carries an unhyphenated suffix was reported missing while sitting in the tree, and an Arabic numeral was dropped from the comparison set entirely. Both helpers were used by nothing else; they are replaced by one _numeral_key applied to both sides — 27 fewer lines, and the bug is no longer expressible.
